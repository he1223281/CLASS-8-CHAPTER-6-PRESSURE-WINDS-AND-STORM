Context: Rabindranath Tagore gave up his knighthood in 1919 in protest against this massacre. The textbook mentions his surrendering it at the start of Non-Co-operation (next act).

Ask: "How did a few thousand British officials rule millions of Indians?" (Indians worked in their offices, courts, police and army, studied in their schools and bought their goods.) "So what happens if Indians stop?" That is the idea of non-co-operation.

The Swashasan → Swarajya shift is the textbook's key point: the demand changed from self-government within the Empire to independence.

Act 08 · Build ours · refuse theirs

Constructive aspect

what to build

  • Strengthen Hindu-Muslim unity
  • Use Swadeshi goods; a spinning wheel in every home
  • Collect ₹1 crore for the Tilak Swarajya Fund
  • Abolish untouchability; national education; prohibition (no liquor)
+

Negative aspect

what to give up

  • Government jobs and titles
  • Government schools and colleges
  • Legislatures, government courts, local self-government
  • Foreign cloth and goods; government functions

If students leave government schools, why must national schools be opened at the same time?

"Negative" here does not mean bad. It means the things people were asked to stop doing (boycott). "Constructive" means the things they were asked to build.

Expected answer: otherwise boycott leaves students with nowhere to study. Building and refusing must go together (compare Swadeshi + Boycott in 1905).

BonfiresForeign clothes were set on fire in many places

Imports of cloth, footwear and luxury goods from England fell sharply. The British Parliament was rattled by the loss.

The government struck back: lathi-charges, firing, mass arrests and torture. Attempts to break Hindu-Muslim unity failed.

Gujarat link: Gujarat Vidyapith was founded by Gandhiji in Ahmedabad in 1920 (context).

Textbook detail: it writes "Duke of Kainatt … boycotted (1921, November)". The Duke of Connaught visited in early 1921, and the Prince of Wales arrived in November 1921 to a nationwide hartal. Both visits were boycotted.

Moplah Revolt (Malabar, 1921): the textbook says it was "between Hindu landlords and Muslim peasants", "deserves criticism", and was suppressed by the British army. Handle it carefully: explain that there was land-related and communal violence, which the national movement condemned, and avoid blaming any whole community.

→

→
DECISIONGandhiji suspended the movement immediately

“I have committed a Himalayan blunder by giving the tool of Satyagraha in the hands of people who do not understand the value of non-violence.”

GANDHIJI · AS QUOTED IN THE TEXTBOOK

Was Gandhiji right to stop a huge movement because of one violent event? What would you have decided?

Context date: 5 February 1922. Most sources give 22 or 23 policemen killed; the textbook says 21. Use the textbook figure for exams.

Run this as a short debate without a verdict. Gandhiji's view: non-violence was the heart of satyagraha, and a violent movement would lose its moral strength and invite crushing repression. Critics' view: the movement lost its momentum just when it was strongest.

Act 09 · Rise and fall
1926DECLINE

Why it mattered

  • Worked in a disciplined way and set high parliamentary standards
  • Proved that Indians can rule constitutionally and democratically
  • Attracted the educated class; national awareness rose again

Why it declined

June 1925Death of Chittaranjan Das; the party weakened
SplitSome members supported the government; others formed the National Party
1926Lost badly in the election, except in Madras Province
CriticsBipin Chandra Pal and Surendranath Banerjee criticised it strongly

Ask: what lesson does the Swarajya Party's story teach about political movements? (Leadership and unity matter. When the leader died and members split, the party lost its strength.)

Link back to Act 1: one reason 1857 failed was the lack of organised leadership. The same weakness appears again here.
